Two Truths and a Lie

Students write three statements about themselves. Classmates ask questions before guessing which one is made up.

Groups of 3–4Paper + pens
Time left
20:00

Explain & model

Start when the class is ready.

On the board

Prompt 1

A few useful phrases

“How often do you ___?” · “When did you ___?” · “I think ___ is the lie because ___.”

How to run it

Before you start

Choose a topic such as food, hobbies or school. The statements on the page are examples to replace. Students can use an invented character if they prefer.

The 20 minutes

  1. 0–3 min
    Play one round together

    Give three statements of your own: two true, one invented. Let the class ask a question before guessing.

  2. 3–9 min
    Write three statements

    Students write their own. Help them phrase anything they’re unsure about and give them time to practise saying it.

  3. 9–16 min
    Ask, then guess

    Take turns in groups. Each person reads their statements. The others ask questions, make a guess, and hear the answer.

  4. 16–20 min
    Remember something true

    Students tell a partner one true thing they learnt. Ask for a few examples of questions that helped.

Worth keeping in mind

Don’t let it become three statements followed by an instant guess. The questions are the useful part.

The English they’re using

Writing short statements · Asking questions · Listening for clues

Make it easier or harder

Keep it simple: Start with “I like…”, “I can…” and “I have…”

Take it further: Use past experiences and ask two questions before guessing.
